• Encode the message clearly • Select a communication method • Send the message • Confirm that the message was

The receiver's responsibility
• Make sure that the information is received in its entirety,
• Decode the message • Confirm that the message was
understood

## PM10:19

Communication methods
• Push communication • Sent a specific information to specific recipients. Examples are letters, memos, e-mails, faxes etc.
• This ensures that message is sent but will not certify that it is actually received or understood.
• Pull communication • Used for large volume of information and large audiences. Examples are internet sites, company data bases, e-learning etc.
• Recipients has to access the communication content.
• Interactive communication • Information are pushed and pulled in real time between two or more parties in multidirectional (Phone calls, meetings, interviews…).

## PM10:47

Manage Stakeholder Engagement
• What: communicating and working with stakeholders to meet their needs and expectations, address issues, and foster appropriate stakeholder involvement
• Why:
• Ensure that stakeholders clearly understand the project goals, objectives, benefits, and risks for the project, as well as how their contribution will enhance project success
• allows the project manager to increase support and minimize resistance from stakeholders
• When: performed throughout the project

## PM10:48

Manage Stakeholder Engagement
• How?
• Engaging stakeholders at appropriate project stages to obtain, confirm, or maintain their continued commitment to the success of the project;
• Managing stakeholder expectations through negotiation and communication;
• Addressing any risks or potential concerns related to stakeholder management and anticipating future issues that may be raised by stakeholders
• Issues • Changes • Risks
• Clarifying and resolving issues that have been identified

engagement.

Direct and Manage Project Work
What?
• The process of leading and performing the work defined in the project management plan and
• Implementing approved changes to achieve the project’s objectives.
Why?
• It provides overall management of the project work and deliverables, thus improving the probability of project success.
When?

Popular PMIS
PMIS capacibilities
• Work breakdown structure (WBS) planning
• Timeline and scheduling (Gantt, roadmap)
• Task tracking and progress management
• Resource assignment and permissions • Resource management • KPI reports, burndown charts • Integration with development tools
(Git, CI/CD) (sometimes)

PMIS limitations
• Real-time communication (chat, video): Not effective — use Slack, Discord, or Teams
• Document writing and collaboration: Better with Google Docs, Confluence
• Source code management & code review: Use GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket
• CI/CD and test automation: Use Jenkins, GitHub Actions, GitLab CI
• Knowledge base or team wiki: Available but often limited — better with Notion or Confluence
