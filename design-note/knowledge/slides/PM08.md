## PM08:4

What is Risk
• A risk is any uncertain event or condition that
might affect your project.
• Event risk is specific uncertain occurrence that could potentially impact the project. (Ex. The lead developer might resign during the project.)
• Condition risk refers to a known or ongoing state or condition that could lead to problems in the future. (Ex. The team is unfamiliar with the new frontend framework selected for the project.)
• Risk may be negative or positive • Negative risks (threats): These can harm your project. (Ex. Vendor delay in delivering critical API integration) • Positive risks (opportunities): These can benefit your project if managed properly. (Ex. New AI tool may accelerate test cycles and reduce workload)

Related terms
Risk Trigger A risk trigger is an event or situation that indicates that a risk is about to occur or has occurred. We can think of risk triggers as warning signs or risk symptoms.

Issue An issue is a risk that has occurred. It is no longer a possibility of harm, loss or gain, so we cannot call it a risk anymore. Instead, it has converted from being a risk to being an issue. Issues are often recorded in the project Issue log.

Overall project risk is the effect of uncertainty on the project as a whole, arising from all sources of uncertainty including individual risks, both positive and negative.

Plan Risk Management
• What: process of defining how to conduct risk management activities for a project
• Why: ensures that the degree, type, and visibility of risk management are proportionate to both risks and the importance of the project to the organization and other stakeholders.
• When: performed once or at predefined points in the project.
• Should begin when a project is conceived and should be completed early in the project.
• May revisit later, for example at a major phase change, or if the project scope changes significantly.

Risk probability and impacts

Risk Probability The likelihood that a specific risk will occur. • Scale: Typically expressed
qualitatively (e.g., Low, Medium, High) or quantitatively (e.g., 0% to 100%). • Example: "There is a 70% chance that supplier delay will occur."

Risk Impact The extent of consequences or effects if the risk occurs. Impact can be measured in terms of: • Cost (e.g., $20,000 over budget) • Time (e.g., 2-week delay) • Scope/Quality (e.g., reduced
features, more defects) • Reputation, safety, etc.

Tools & Techniques: Data Analysis

Root cause analysis with cause and effect diagrams – A technique designed to uncover the underlying risk. It often uses cause and effect diagrams such as fishbone diagrams.

Steps: 1. Define the Risk: write the risk at the “head” of the diagram. 2. Identify Major Categories: identify the main categories that contribute to the risk. 3. Brainstorm Contributing Factors: write potential causes that contribute to the risk. 4. Analyze the Diagram: identify key contributing factors. 5. Prioritize Risks: assign priorities based on their likelihood and impact. 6. Develop Action Plans: develop actionable plans to mitigate or eliminate risks.

Tools & Techniques: Data Analysis

Assumption and constraint analysis – Project estimates of benefits and value delivered are based on assumptions and constraints. This activity examines these assumptions and constraints and asks what might jeopardize them or make them invalid. This can help identify risks.

Assumption: A factor in the planning process that is considered to be true, real, or certain, without proof or demonstration.

## PM08:27

Tools & Techniques: Data Analysis
SWOT analysis – SWOT is an acronym for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats. SWOT Analysis examines the project from each of these perspectives starting with the strengths and weaknesses of the project, organization and business area in general. From the strengths, opportunities are investigated, and the weaknesses examined as a potential source of threats.

• What: process of numerically analyzing the combined effect of identified individual project risks and other sources of uncertainty on overall project objectives.
• Why: It quantifies overall project risk exposure, and it can also provide additional quantitative risk information to support risk response planning.
• When: performed throughout the project (not required for all projects).

• Interpreting the outputs of quantitative risk analysis.

## PM08:59

Plan Risk Responses
• What: process of developing options, selecting strategies, and agreeing on actions to address overall project risk exposure, as well as to treat individual project risks.
• Why: identifies appropriate ways to address overall project risk and individual
project risks • Allocates resources and inserts activities into project documents and the project management plan as needed. • Effective and appropriate risk responses can minimize individual threats, maximize individual opportunities, and reduce overall project risk exposure.
• When: performed throughout the project • How:
• Risk responses should be appropriate for the significance of the risk, costeffective in meeting the challenge, realistic within the project context, agreed upon by all parties involved, and owned by a responsible person.
• Specific actions are developed to implement the agreed-upon risk response strategy, including primary and backup strategies, as necessary.

Strategies for Threads

• Escalate – Transfer to someone outside the project. Convince the powers that be, that this risk is significant enough that it should be handled at the program or portfolio level.
• Avoid – take evasive action. Find a way to eliminate the source of the risk. Maybe use a different approach or technology that does not contain the risk.

• Transfer - Move the risk to a group better equipped to handle it. This can include taking out insurance which exchanges exposure for a known premium.
• Mitigate- Make the risk smaller. Through actions, reduce the probability of the risk occurring or the impact to the project should the risk occur.
• Accept – For low priority risks, or those we cannot avoid or reduce, we may have to accept the risk. Here the best thing we can do might be to create a contingency reserve to help deal with the risk should it occur

## PM08:64

Strategies for Opportunities

Escalate – Transfer to someone better able to ensure the opportunity occurs. Maybe someone with more influence or authority at the program, portfolio and sponsor level. Exploit – Try to drive the probability up to 100% and maximize impact. Perhaps by assigning our best people to it early and giving them everything they need to ensure it occurs.

Share – Transfer the opportunity to others, either inside our organization or outside to maximize the value derived and captured. Enhance – Similar to exploit, but to a lesser degree, try to increase the probability and impact. Focus attention on the causes and prioritize this work for early delivery. Accept – Sometimes, there is not much we can do to increase the likelihood of an opportunity occurring (such as good weather). All we can do is be ready and prepared to take advantage of it, so it does not pass us by unrealized.

## PM08:72

Implement Risk Responses
• What: process of implementing agreed-upon risk response plans.
• A common problem with Project Risk Management is that project teams spend effort in identifying and analyzing risks and developing risk responses, then risk responses are agreed upon and documented in the risk register and risk report, but no action is taken to manage the risk.
• Only if risk owners give the required level of effort to implementing the agreed-upon responses will the overall risk exposure of the project and individual threats and opportunities be managed proactively.
• Why: ensures that agreed-upon risk responses are executed as planned in order to
• address overall project risk exposure. • minimize individual project threats, and maximize individual project
opportunities
• When: performed throughout the project

## PM08:77

Risk response can find even more risks

Secondary risks come from a response you have to another risk. If you dig a trench to stop landslides from taking out your camp, it’s possible for someone to fall into the trench and get hurt

Residual risks remain after your risk responses have been implemented. So even though you reinforce your tent stakes and get weatherproof gear, there’s still a chance that winds could destroy your camp if they are strong enough.

## PM08:102

Tools & Techniques

Alternatives analysis.
• Assess the requested changes and decide which are accepted, rejected, or need to be modified to be finally accepted.
Cost-benefit analysis.
• Determine if the requested change is worth its associated cost.
Decision making
• To decide on whether to accept, defer, or reject change requests. – Voting – Autocratic decision making – Multicriteria decision analysis.

Meetings
• A change control board (CCB) is responsible for meeting and Reviewing the change requests and approving, deferring or rejecting those change requests.
• The CCB may meet only once a week, once every other week, or even once a month, depending on the project.
• When emergencies arise, the preestablished procedures allow the project manager to implement the change on the spot.
• The CCB may also review configuration management activities.

## PM08:103

Outputs

Approved Change Requests
• As a result, changes may be approved, deferred, or rejected.
• Approved change requests will be implemented through the Direct and Manage Project Work process.
• Deferred or rejected change requests are communicated to the person or group requesting the change.
• The status of all the changes, approved or not, will be updated in the change log as part of the project document updates

Project Management Plan Updates
• Changes to baselines are only made from the last baseline forward.
• Past performance is not changed.
Project Document Updates
• Change log: Log or record of changes made to a project
• Any documents that are subject to the formal change control process

## PM08:104

Preventing or correcting problems
In both corrective and preventive action, you always need to submit your proposed change and put it through the Perform Integrated Change Control
process—and only if it is approved will you implement it.
